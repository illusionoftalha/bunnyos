Roots and Ruins

I did not ask to arrive the way I did —
tearing open a woman I never got to meet,
my first sound a cry,
my first inheritance: his blame.
He looked at my face and saw her grave.
He could not separate the living from the loss.
So he left.
Not slowly, not with a backward glance —
just gone, the way a candle goes
when no one bothers to shield it from the wind.
And I grew in the dark,
learning the shape of a door
by the sound of it closing.
✦
They gave me a home, the ones who took me in.
Warm food, a bed, a name they called at dinner.
They held me the way people hold
something borrowed — carefully,
but always knowing it belongs somewhere else.
I felt it in the softness of their love:
how it had edges. How it could be folded
and put away. How they loved me
and still — would let me go.
I told myself: this is enough.
I told myself: stop reaching.
I planted myself in their garden
and tried to bloom,
tried to stop listening
for footsteps that never came.
✦
Then — ten years later — the door opened.
Not with an apology.
Not with outstretched arms or wet eyes.
Just him. Standing there.
Alive.
As if living had been a choice he made about himself
and had nothing to do with me.
He was alive this whole time.
He was breathing while I was learning
that fathers are something other children have.
He was eating, sleeping, existing —
while I pressed my ear to the silence
and called it peace.
Nineteen years old and I am standing
in the same wound I was born into,
just larger now. Just older.
The bleeding doesn't stop —
it just learns to be quiet.
✦
He came back not to hold me
but to move me.
Send her somewhere new, he said,
somewhere far, somewhere she knows no one.
Cut the threads. Sever the roots.
Make her life a room with no windows.
As if the first abandonment wasn't enough —
he wants to do it clean this time,
with paperwork.
And the ones who said they loved me
are already packing my things.
Not fighting. Not saying: no.
Just helping with the tape and the boxes
like love was always a loan
and now the lender has returned.
✦
How do you grieve the living?
How do you mourn a man
who stands in front of you
and is still — somehow — absent?
How do you trust a warmth
that always comes with an expiry?
How many times can a girl be left
before leaving becomes the only thing she knows?
I don't know what it feels like
to be chosen.
I only know what it feels like
to be returned.
Gently. Politely. With an explanation
that was supposed to make it easier.
It never did.
✦
But I am still here.
God, I am still here.
Not because anyone stayed —
but because something in me
refused to leave myself too.
Some small, stubborn, unbroken thing
in the centre of all this wreckage
that still — impossibly — stands.
I am the daughter of a mother
I was never allowed to meet.
I am the child of a man
who chose his grief over his girl.
I am the girl who was borrowed and returned,
who was loved in halves,
who was sent away like a letter
no one wanted to keep receiving.
And I am still —
still —
standing.
Not because the world held me.
Because I learned to hold myself.
And that — though it cost everything —
is a kind of miracle.
