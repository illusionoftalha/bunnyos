Bhonduu

There are people who pass through like weather,
and then there's him — steady, like ground beneath a storm.
I have learned the difference the hard way,
counting all the ones who left before they were needed,
and here he stays, unmoved, unshaken,
tall like a tree that has decided
it will not bend for anything less than the truth.
He does not know his own height.
He looks in mirrors and sees an average man,
some forgettable face in a forgettable crowd —
and I want to shake him by the shoulders and say
look again, look harder, look the way I look,
because I have never seen a man more whole,
more rare, more finely made,
and he wears it all so quietly,
like he doesn't know he's the loudest kind of good
in a world that mostly settles for less.
He dresses in formals, sharp lines, composed collars,
and still — still — the softness leaks through anyway,
in the way he holds a door,
in the way he lowers his voice when I'm tired,
in the way gentleness looks strange on a man
who could carry the weight of the room
and instead chooses to carry mine.
When he walks in, the room feels less crowded somehow.
It isn't dramatic, it isn't a scene pulled from a film —
it's just that my eyes catch him first, out of habit,
out of comfort, the way you spot something familiar
in a sea of strangers without even trying.
My world doesn't explode into color,
it just settles, like the noise finally makes sense.
And that smile —
God, that smile has undone better people than me.
The way a small dimple hides in his cheek
like a secret he doesn't know he's keeping,
the way tiny lines form under his eyes when he laughs,
soft creases of a joy that has no performance in it,
no mask, no posture — just him,
childlike, unguarded, easy,
like laughter is the one language
he never had to learn.
And when he really laughs, the loud kind,
the kind that catches him off guard —
it doesn't sound like a sound,
it sounds like an ocean deciding to be gentle,
waves that could have been violent
choosing instead to be kind,
crashing softly just to make me smile too.
He is not like the others.
I have known guys who mistake volume for strength,
who mistake indifference for cool,
but he — he checks if I'm okay before he checks anything else.
He cannot stand to see a frown settle on my face.
It unsettles something in him,
some quiet panic he tries to hide,
some fear that he might be the reason,
though he is never, ever the reason —
he is the reason things get better.
He treats my feelings like something breakable and precious,
handles them like glass wrapped in his own two palms,
careful, so careful,
like he has decided, somewhere along the way,
that my peace is worth protecting
even from himself.
He gave me a name.
Not my name — a smaller one, a softer one,
one that only exists between us,
one that makes the world feel less enormous
every time he says it.
He shows up.
Not when it's convenient, not when it's easy —
he shows up when I need him,
which is somehow always exactly when he arrives,
like he has some quiet radar for my breaking points,
like distance never really mattered to him at all.
I don't know how to explain what he is to me
without sounding like I'm writing about the sun —
too bright to look at directly,
too warm to stay away from,
too constant to ever take for granted.
⸻
So here it is, Bhonduu,
written the only way I know how to say it —
not perfectly, not without my hands shaking a little,
but honestly, fully, the way you deserve:
you are the realest thing I've ever known.
Not because you're flawless —
but because even with all your doubts about yourself,
you still choose, every single day,
to be gentle in a world that rarely asks you to be.
That's rare.
That's you.
That's Bhonduu.
