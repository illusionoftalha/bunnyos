No One Stayed

I. — the ones who left while breathing

They left the way seasons leave —
without saying goodbye,
without looking back
at the door they left open,
at the one still standing in it,
still waiting.
I have sat at tables full of people
and been the most alone thing in the room.
I have smiled at the right moments,
laughed when they expected it,
and walked home to a silence
that knew my name better than any of them.
The ones I loved the most
left the deepest.
Not with cruelty —
with something worse:
indifference.
· · ·
II. — the one who is in the grave

And the one who died —
God, the one who died —
they took with them
the only version of me
that felt like it belonged somewhere.
I still talk to you.
Not to the grave —
I cannot go there,
it would destroy me completely —
but to the air in the room,
to the side of the bed
where you used to sit,
to the cup I still make for two
before I remember.
I remember how you said my name —
like it meant something,
like I meant something,
like the world would be smaller
without me in it.
The world did not become smaller.
It became louder.
And I became quieter.
And no one noticed the trade.
I still yearn for you
the way a wound yearns
for the hand that would have healed it —
knowing that hand is gone,
still reaching,
still reaching,
still reaching.
· · ·
III. — the blood no one saw

I have been bleeding
for so long
that I forgot
it wasn't normal.
I bled in the middle of conversations.
I bled through dinners and celebrations.
I bled through birthdays that no one called about.
I bled through the nights
when the dark felt less like darkness
and more like a living thing
pressing against my chest.
And no one saw it.
No one looked at my shoulder
and saw the wound there,
old and untended,
the kind that doesn't close
because no one ever thought
to ask if you were hurting.
They looked right at me
and saw nothing.
Or they saw,
and decided
it was not their problem.
I bled until dying felt like resting.
I want you to understand that.
Not the darkness of it —
the exhaustion.
The sheer unbearable weight
of carrying yourself
through a world that lets you
do it alone.
· · ·
IV. — the blame laid on innocent hands

They gave me the blame
before they gave me a name.
Before I knew what I had done,
I was already guilty of it.
Every room I entered,
I entered carrying it —
their disappointments,
their failures,
their grief they refused to hold
so they handed it to me
and called it my fault.
And I believed them.
That is the part that breaks me
even now —
I believed them.
I spent years
apologizing for being born,
for taking up space,
for needing things,
for being human in front of people
who had no patience for it.
I carried the blame till my back curved.
I carried it till I forgot
what standing straight felt like.
Till I forgot
that some of this was never mine.
I will carry it to my grave
if someone doesn't come
and gently
take it from me.
But no one is coming.
No one ever came.
· · ·
V. — still yearning for the one who abandoned them

And still.
God help me.
Still.
I still look for you in crowds.
I still turn at voices
that sound like yours.
I still dream of the version of you
that chose to stay —
and in the dream you always do,
and in the dream it's always enough,
and I wake up
and the ceiling is just the ceiling again.
You abandoned me.
You knew everything I was carrying
and you left anyway.
You saw the blood on my shoulder
and you looked away.
You heard me ask
and you went quiet.
You were alive and present and able
and you chose, deliberately,
not to.
And I miss you.
I hate that I miss you.
I hate that the heart
does not know
the difference between
someone who was taken
and someone who walked away —
it grieves them both the same,
desperately,
without dignity.
I would forgive you
if you asked.
I would open the door
if you knocked.
Even now.
Even after everything.
That is what love does
to a person —
it leaves them standing at a door
that has been shut for years,
still hopeful,
still ruined,
still knocking.
· · ·
VI. — what remains

So here I stand.
Blamed for things I did not do.
Grieving someone in the ground.
Yearning for someone still breathing
who does not think of me at all.
Bleeding in a room
where no one looks up.
And still —
still —
I wake up every morning.
I make the coffee.
I put on the face.
I carry the weight
no one offered to share.
I fold the blame back into my chest
like a letter
no one was ever going to read.
This is what surviving looks like
when no one is watching.
This is what it means
to love people
who were not careful with you.
This is what it means
to miss someone in a grave
and someone in a city
with equal desperation.
I am innocent.
I need to say that
even if no one is here to hear it.
I was innocent
and I was gentle
and I was trying
and it was not enough
for people who had already decided
they would not be moved by me.
But I was here.
I bled and I loved and I stayed
and I was here.
And somewhere,
in the space between
the grave that took you
and the road that lost you,
I am still here.
Wounded.
Waiting.
Unremarkably, stubbornly
still here.
— written for the innocent ones who carried the weight of other people's leaving
who bled in silence and were never once asked if they were alright
who loved without being loved back, and kept loving anyway
